Wedding dresses in Ho Chi Minh City

Where to go, what it costs, how to book the boutiques and what to sort before you fly. Written for Australian brides considering the trip, from one I did in October 2026.

Before you go

The admin is genuinely small. The visa is the only real task, and it is done from your couch.

The e-visa

Australians need a visa, and the e-visa is the easy route. It is entirely online, no embassy appointment, no passport posted anywhere. It runs up to 90 days and can be single or multiple entry.

  • Around US$25 for single entry, plus a small card fee
  • Roughly three working days, though people report a week, so give yourself a fortnight
  • You upload a photo of your passport page and a plain portrait shot; neither needs to be professional
  • Print the approved PDF on A4 and hand it over with your passport at immigration
Watch for this

There are dozens of copycat visa sites that look official and charge several times the real fee. Start from evisa.immigration.gov.vn, the Immigration Department's own portal, or from the link on Smartraveller. The official site looks dated. That is the correct one.

How far out to go

I went eight months before the wedding, and that felt right. My dress is being made now and ships at the end of March, about three months before a June wedding, which leaves room for alterations at home without living in a panic about it.

Anything tighter than six months and you are relying on everything going perfectly: the making, the shipping, and your own alterations once it lands. Eight is comfortable.

Booking the appointments

There are no booking forms and email is not the channel. Everything happens in Instagram DMs and WhatsApp, and it works far better than it sounds.

How it works

Find the boutique on Instagram, message the account, and you will usually hear back the same day. Some prefer you move the conversation to WhatsApp once you've said hello. Most reply in English.

Hours run late. Many are open from 10am until 8 or 8.30pm, seven days a week, so two appointments in one day is comfortable, and three is possible if they're in the same district.

What they'll ask you for

Every boutique asked for the same five things, so have them ready and paste them into the first message:

  • Your name
  • A phone number
  • Your wedding date
  • Preferred appointment time
  • How many guests you're bringing
Worth knowing

A few will ask whether you're arriving by car so they can talk you through parking. The streets are narrow and most studios have no frontage at all. You will be walking down an alley and up a staircase. That is normal and not a red flag.

Keep Google Translate open

Not everyone speaks English, and the moments where that matters are the ones you care about: explaining exactly what you want changed, or understanding what is and isn't possible.

Google Translate bridged it beautifully. Typing a sentence and turning the phone around became completely normal within about an hour, and nobody minded in the slightest. Download the Vietnamese pack before you fly so it works without signal.

How many to book

I booked four and went to two. I found my dress early, which meant cancelling the back half of the week. A good problem, and one worth planning for.

Book more than you think you need, spread across the week rather than crammed into two days, and be relaxed about cancelling. Everyone was gracious about it. Leaving the back end of the trip loose also means you can go back to a favourite instead of deciding under pressure.

Getting around & money

Grab, not taxis

I used Grab to get everywhere. Cheap, safe and easy. Download it before you fly and link a card. The price is fixed before you accept, so there is no haggling and no wondering about the meter.

Book the car, not the bike, on appointment days. You will be in something you don't want to arrive sweaty in, and the traffic is relentless.

Where things are

Base yourself in District 1. The central boutiques are a short walk or a two-minute ride from each other around Bến Thành.

Anything outside that, such as Hòa Hưng or Bình Thạnh, is a 20 to 30 minute Grab depending on traffic, and traffic is usually the answer. Group your out-of-district appointments on the same day.

Costs & ordering

The honest numbers, and what happens after you say yes.

What a dress costs

For a custom dress at the more elevated, intricate end, expect roughly A$2,800 to A$4,000, with A$4,000 being the top of the range rather than the middle of it.

That is for something made for you, not altered off a rack. Set that against what the same level of work costs at home and the trip starts paying for itself fairly quickly.

Going to a tailor rather than a bridal house changes the maths entirely. My second dress was A$680. Less hand beading and no boutique behind it, but beautifully made. Worth knowing both ends of the range exist.

Deposits and timeline

You pay a deposit on the day and the dress is made after you fly home. Mine was ordered in October and ships at the end of March, so about five months of making, and you are trusting people you have met once.
