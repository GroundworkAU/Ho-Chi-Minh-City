Wedding dresses in Ho Chi Minh City

Where to go, what it costs, how to book the boutiques and what to sort before you fly. I did this trip in October 2026, and this is everything I'd tell a friend who was thinking about doing the same.

Before you go

There isn't much to sort before you go. The visa is the only real job, and you can do the whole thing from the couch.

The e-visa

Australians need a visa and the e-visa is the easiest way to do it. It's all online, so there's no embassy appointment and you don't have to post your passport anywhere. It runs up to 90 days, single or multiple entry.

  • Around US$25 for single entry, plus a small card fee
  • Roughly three working days, but I've seen people say a week, so give yourself a fortnight
  • You upload a photo of your passport page and a plain portrait shot, neither of which needs to be professional
  • Print the approved PDF on A4 and hand it over with your passport at immigration
Watch for this

There are so many copycat visa sites that look official and charge you several times the real fee. Start from evisa.immigration.gov.vn, which is the Immigration Department's own portal, or use the link on Smartraveller. The official one looks a bit dated, which is how you know you're in the right place.

How far out to go

I'd say eight to twelve months out is good timing. It gives the dress enough time to be made, shipped and then altered at home without everything being a rush at the end.

I went at eight months and it worked. My dress is being made now and ships at the end of March, which is about three months before our June wedding. If you can go a little earlier, do it, because it takes the pressure off the back end.

Booking the appointments

There are no booking forms and nobody really uses email. It all happens through Instagram DMs and WhatsApp, which works so much better than it sounds.

How it works

Find the boutique on Instagram, message the account and you'll usually hear back the same day. Some will move you over to WhatsApp once you've said hello. Most of them reply in English.

They're open late too, most of them 10am until 8 or 8.30pm seven days a week, so you can easily do two appointments in a day and three if they're in the same district.

What they'll ask you for

Every boutique asked me for the same five things, so have these ready to paste into your first message:

  • Your name
  • A phone number
  • Your wedding date
  • Preferred appointment time
  • How many guests you're bringing

Ask them two things back in the same message: what size their samples are, and whether they're taking custom orders at the moment. Both change from boutique to boutique, and both can decide whether the appointment is worth your time.

Keep Google Translate open

Not everyone speaks English, and the moments where it matters are the ones you really care about, like explaining exactly what you want changed or understanding what is and isn't possible.

Always have Google Translate handy. I found it such a great way to communicate, and typing something out and turning your phone around feels completely normal after about an hour. Download the Vietnamese pack before you fly so it works without signal.

How many to book

I booked four and went to two, because I found my dress early and ended up cancelling the back half of the week. A good problem to have, but worth planning for.

Book more than you think you need and spread them across the week rather than cramming them into two days. Everyone was lovely about me cancelling. Leaving the back end of your trip a bit loose also means you can go back to a favourite instead of making a decision under pressure.

Haute couture and the most formal of everywhere I went. Structured strapless bodices, enormous satin and lace skirts, long trains and veils. They sell in tiered packages rather than single gowns, so ask what each one includes before you fall in love with something.

Phuong Giang Tailor @phuonggiangtailor

Second dress, A$680

A tailor rather than a bridal store, and the best value of the whole trip. I had my second dress made here for A$680, and some other pieces while I was at it, jackets and trousers, and every single one came back incredible. Highly recommend.

They do custom pieces in 24 to 48 hours, so go early in your trip rather than at the end. Heritage tailoring is their thing, so áo dài and cheongsam alongside modern cuts, which is worth knowing if anyone else in your wedding party needs something made.

The process was so simple. I showed them photos and videos of a dress I liked, they measured me and quoted me off the back of that. It took about a week to make and they shipped it over to me.

It's gorgeous and so well made, but it did come up a bit big. I'd expected it wouldn't fit perfectly and I was always going to get it tailored here anyway, so that's worth factoring in: budget for a local alteration at this end rather than expecting it to arrive ready to wear.

Getting around & money

Grab, not taxis

I used Grab to book taxis everywhere. Cheap, safe and easy. Download it before you fly and link a card. The price is set before you accept, so there's no haggling and no wondering about the meter.

Book the car and not the bike on appointment days. You'll be in something you don't want to turn up sweaty in, and the traffic is relentless.

Where things are

Base yourself in District 1. The central boutiques are a short walk or a two minute ride from each other around Bến Thành.

Anything outside that, like Hòa Hưng or Bình Thạnh, is a 20 to 30 minute Grab depending on traffic, and there's usually traffic. Try to group your out-of-district appointments on the same day.

Paying the boutiques

The bridal boutiques don't take cash or card. You pay your deposit by international bank transfer, and that's the one thing I'd most want to know before going.

Sort it out before you fly. Work out how you'd send an international transfer, what your bank charges and how long it takes to clear, so that saying yes to a dress isn't followed by an afternoon of banking admin in your hotel room.

The tailor is different

Phuong Giang takes cash or card. It's only the bridal boutiques that work on transfer.

Phone

Get an eSIM before you leave. You'll be following Grab pins down alleys and messaging boutiques on WhatsApp all day, and hotel wifi is no help once you're out.

Costs & ordering

What it actually costs, and what happens after you say yes.

What a dress costs

For a custom dress at the more elevated, intricate end, you're looking at around A$2,800 to A$4,000, with A$4,000 being the very top end rather than the middle.

That's for something made for you rather than altered off a rack. Put it against what the same work costs at home and the trip starts paying for itself pretty quickly.

Going to a tailor instead of a bridal house changes the maths completely. My second dress was A$680. Less hand beading and no boutique behind it, but beautifully made. Worth knowing both ends of the range are there.

Deposits and timeline

You pay a deposit on the day and the dress gets made after you fly home. Mine was ordered in October and ships at the end of March, so about five months of making, and you're trusting people you've met once.

My deposit at Uni Bridal was 50%, with the balance due before the dress ships. The tailor was different again: I paid in full when I ordered.

The deposit goes by international bank transfer rather than cash or card, so have that worked out before your first appointment.

Fittings and measurements

At the tailor it was refreshingly simple. They measured me there and then, quoted me off the photos I'd brought, and that was the whole process. No back and forth.

For the bridal dress you're measured at the appointment and the dress is made after you fly home, so there's no second fitting unless you go back. That's the trade-off with ordering overseas, and it's the reason to leave time for alterations at this end.

Getting it home

Both of mine are shipped rather than carried home. The tailor's dress took about a week to make and they posted it over to me. It's gorgeous and so well made, but it came up a bit big.

I'd expected that and had always planned to have it tailored here, which is the bit I'd pass on: budget for an alteration in Australia rather than expecting anything to arrive ready to wear. My Uni Bridal dress is still being made and ships at the end of March, so I'll know more then.

All based on my trip in October 2026. Prices, hours and visa rules change, so check the official sources before you book anything.
